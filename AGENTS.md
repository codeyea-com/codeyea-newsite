### Dimensions are the first priority

- Before designing an image, measure its original intended section frame at desktop, tablet and mobile widths. Record displayed width, height, aspect ratio, responsive changes, cropping behavior and any overlays or motion.
- Design the image composition for that actual frame from the outset. A matching file aspect ratio or unchanged HTML frame dimensions alone does not prove a successful fit.
- Check the size of the meaningful visual content within the image, not just the bitmap's outer bounds. Embedded margins, empty backgrounds and tiny screenshots must not make the subject look undersized inside the frame.
- Do not place a landscape composition in a portrait frame with empty space above and below. Do not fill a required wide frame with a distant, tiny subject surrounded by meaningless space.
- Do not fix an unsuitable composition by stretching it, adding blank padding, hiding important content through cropping, or changing the approved section dimensions. Plan a correctly composed asset and responsive variant when necessary within authorized scope.
- Verify the actual result visually inside the complete section with its title, explanation and surrounding elements, at each relevant viewport and interaction state. Assess visible scale, meaningful framing, legibility, crop and balance with the text.
- Show the original/reference and proposed result side by side inside equivalent frames before claiming the dimensions or composition are correct.

### Content and visual meaning

- Study CODEYEA's actual page and section copy, audience, service, business problem and intended message before choosing a visual concept.
- CODEYEA provides digital services to businesses. Imagery must explain the relevant service or the business context described by the section.
- A Fashion industry page addresses fashion business owners and their work. The owner's original example shows an owner organizing designs, samples and work behind the store. Unrelated fashion models posing in an empty studio fail to communicate that business message.
- On the Ecommerce service page, a handbag product photograph does not explain storefront design; a desktop product page does not explain mobile shopping; a checkout screen does not explain product collections.
- Generic product shots, packing-box scenes or administration screenshots are not acceptable merely because they relate loosely to ecommerce. Each concept needs a specific, defensible connection to the section's title and explanation.
- Never generate images simply to fill space or finish the task. Both the concept and the image quality must succeed.

### Required research before design

- Once the owner explicitly authorizes research, first study CODEYEA's pages and sections in context.
- Review corresponding pages and sections at https://www.hostinger.com/ and https://world.siteground.com/, including the imagery beside its actual content and any relevant interactions. A homepage glance or text-only summary is insufficient visual research.
- When these references do not cover a CODEYEA topic, find relevant reputable, specialist and creative sites that do. Select references by content relevance.
- Present findings before design or generation: CODEYEA page/section, intended message, existing-image assessment, original frame measurements, reference URL and visual example, proposed concept, why it fits the content and frame, and applicable responsive considerations.
- Explain what is learned from each reference and how that serves CODEYEA's identity. Do not copy an unrelated reference composition simply for its appearance.

### Image quality and uniqueness

- Require photographic realism in faces, hair, skin, leather, wood, fabric and other materials, with believable detail and lighting.
- Use true, natural colors and neutral whites. Avoid a pervasive off-white cast, artificial color wash, painterly AI texture or synthetic grading. Natural shadows and material shading remain appropriate.

- Do not use the em dash character U+2014 in newly written copy or responses.
- Browser/automated checks are supporting evidence only. Do not describe technical checks as proof of visual quality, semantic relevance or owner approval.
